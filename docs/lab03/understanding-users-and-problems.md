PHASE 2:
  WHAT:
    Primary group: current university students
    When and where: When they are looking for events to attend, happens the most during busy times.
    Goals and needs: We are trying to improve the effort it takes to discover events, and avoid missing opportunities. The main achievement is creating a centralized place for students to find events
    Current behaviour/ workaround: They get email of events that could get lost in their spam or may not be relevant to then, asking their classmates and friends or being informed by them about the events happening, they also get informed from multiple social media platforms.
    Possible pain points: effort to find emails through the spam emails and some events that students are not interested in which could confuse them, risk of costing event mangers money when not enough people show up.
  

PHASE 3:


  We will investigate UDST students who attend or look for university events to learn how students currently discover events, what information they need, and what difficulties they experience.
  
  
  Methods
  
  
  Method 1. Reviewing existing solutions 
      eg. The existing Navigate360 events section, including the information provided and how events are displayed.
  
     
  Method 2. Asking students by sending out a survey.
     
  
  The survey will be asking about : 
  
  
   1. Tell me about the last university event you attended or considered attending. How did you find out about it?
  
     
   2. What information did you look for before deciding to attend?
  
     
   3. What did you do when you could not find enough information?
  
     
   4. Have you ever missed an event because you found out about it too late? What happened?
  
    
   5. Have you used Navigate360 to look for events? What was your experience?
  


PHASE 4:

EVIDENCE 1: REVIEW 


  Source: The existing UDST Navigate360 events section.
  Findings:
  •	Very basic list of events
  
  •	It lacks advanced filtering options
  
  •	It lacks student ratings or opinions
  
  •	It lacks visual elements like event pictures
  
  •	It lacks attendance counts
  
  •	It lacks clear tags showing details such as whether food or participation certificates are provided
  
  •	Small section that is easy to miss
  

EVIDENCE 2: ASK IN A SURVEY

  Question 1 Feedback:
  
  Outlook was the most common source, followed by friends/faculty and social media. Navigate360 and posters/flyers were not reported as       sources.
  
  
  Question 2 Feedback:
  Students mainly look for the date, time, type of event, activities, location, purpose, and benefits. When information is missing,           students commonly check social media, search online, or ask friends/others.
  
  
  Question 3 Feedback:
  Many students were unaware of or had never used Navigate360 to find events. Some had seen it but did not use it, while only a few had       used it and found it easy to find events.
  
  Question 4 Feedback:
  Most students reported that they had missed an interesting event because they found out about it too late.
  
  
  Question 5 Feedback:
  Responses ranged from neutral to negative, with no highly positive ratings. About half of students rated the experience negatively, while   the other half gave neutral ratings.

    
PHASE 5:
  Before this investigation, we thought students mainly struggled with finding events because university events were announced through scattered channels such as Outlook, social media, flyers, and word of mouth, and there was no effective centralized place to discover events.
  
  Our evidence suggests that there is already a centralized event section in Navigate360, but many students are unaware of it or do not use it regularly. Students mainly discover events through Outlook, friends/faculty, and social media, and many have missed events because they found out about them too late. When deciding whether to attend, students look for key information such as the date, time, location, event type, activities, purpose, and benefits. The existing Navigate360 event section provides some event information but has limited discovery features and does not appear to fully meet these needs.
  
  We are now interested in understanding what can be added or improved to help students easily discover relevant events, find the information they need, and receive event information in a way that is useful to them, whether through an improved existing system or a new application.
  
  Because our investigation showed that a centralized event section already exists, but students still rely on multiple channels to discover events, many are unaware of or do not use Navigate360, and most surveyed students have experienced missing events because they found out too late. This suggests an opportunity to explore improvements such as better search and filtering, personalized event discovery, clearer event information, notifications or reminders, and student engagement features.
