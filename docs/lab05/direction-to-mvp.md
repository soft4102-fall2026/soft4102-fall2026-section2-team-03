PHASE 1:
  SELECTED DIRECTION: We will explore a way to make university events easier to discover by bringing event information into a centralized, organized, and accessible experience.
  CANDIDATE SOLUTION: A centralized university event discovery service that brings events from different university sources together into one organized place, making it easier for individuals to find and understand available academic, social, and career events.
  SOFTWARE PRODUCT CONCEPT: A university event discovery platform that provides individuals with one organized place to discover university events and access the key information they need to decide whether to attend.


Phase 2: VALUE


Target user / beneficiary: Current UDST students and faculty who look for events to attend, especially during busy periods such as the start of term and exam weeks.


Expected value: Students and faculty spend less effort finding events and stop missing career, social and academic opportunities because they found out too late.


Expected value statement:
Our product should help UDST students and faculty to find the university events that are worth their time in one place, early enough to attend, so they stop missing career, social and academic opportunities.




PHASE 3 - ASSUMING

Note: Individuals are UDST students, faculty and staff.

1. Key assumption: Individuals would find it easier to discover university events if event information was available in one central            platform.
   
   Why does it matter: Individuals currently discover events through different channels leading to missing many events, so we need to          determine if centralizing event information would make discovery easier.


   If it is wrong: Individuals will continue relying on Outlook, social media, friends, or others and it would still be difficult to find.



2. Key assumption: Individuals can get enough information about an event from the central event listing to decide whether they want to         attend.

   
   Why does it matter: Individuals look for information such as the date, time, location, activities, purpose, and reviews before deciding     whether to attend.



   If it is wrong: Individuals may still need to check other sources or ask others for more information.



   
3. Key assumption: Having events in one central place will reduce the chance of individuals finding out about events too late.

   
   Why does it matter: Many individuals reported missing interesting events because they found out about them too late from a different        source.


   If it is wrong: Centralizing events may make them easier to find but may not solve the problem of late discovery.

PHASE 4:

Part A: CAPABILITIES — What Must the Software Enable
 
Expected Value: Find university events in one place
Software Capability: Enable individuals to discover university events from different university sources in one centralized place.

Expected Value: Find events worth their time
Software Capability: Enable individuals to search and explore events based on information relevant to their interests and needs.

Expected Value: Get enough information to decide whether to attend
Software Capability: Enable individuals to quickly understand the key details of an event, including its date, time, location, purpose, and activities.

Expected Value: Find out about events early enough to attend
Software Capability: Enable individuals to become aware of upcoming events before they occur.

Expected Value: Avoid missing relevant opportunities
Software Capability: Enable individuals to keep track of upcoming events they may want to attend.

Part B: PRIORITIZE — Protect the Core Value

ESSENTIAL FOR CORE VALUE:
1.Find university events in one place
2.Get enough information to decide whether to attend
3. Find out about events early enough to attend

USEFUL, NOT ESSENTIAL YET:
1.Find events worth their time
2.Avoid missing relevant opportunities

LATER: --


PHASE 5: SCOPE

A first version that allows UDST students and faculty to discover upcoming university events in one centralized place, view the key information they need to decide whether to attend and find events early enough to participate.


IN THE MVP:

-Centralized event listing

-Clear event information 

-Basic search/filtering to find events quickly

-Upcoming event visibility to order the events starting with the soonest

NOT YET:

-Personalized event recommendations can be added later

-Student ratings and reviews

-Attendance counts

-Notification and reminder systems


The capabilities kept in are sufficient because they directly address the main problem we identified including students struggle to discover university events, find enough information about them, and learn about them early enough. By putting upcoming events in one place and showing clear key details, the MVP makes event discovery simpler and reduces the need to search through multiple sources. This allows us to test whether a centralized and organized event experience actually helps students find and understand events more easily.
